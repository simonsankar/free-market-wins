# The reply

Mode D only, or when asked. One message, sent to a real person in a real
conversation. Goes in chat, never in the published file. These rules come from
a session where every one of them was learned the hard way.

## Order

1. **Restate their claim fairly**, at full strength, in a sentence or two. If
   they'd quibble with the restatement, it's wrong — fix it before going on.
2. **Establish the shared definition** of the key term, one they'll agree to.
3. **Concede what's true** in what they said.
4. **Walk through where the logic fails**, from the shared definition to the
   hidden premise, with one checkable piece of evidence (history, revealed
   preference, a real case).
5. Stop. No summary of what you just said.

## Rules

- **Plain prose.** No headers, numbered steps, bold "moves," or labelled
  sections. Structure reads as a debate tactic, not a conversation.
- **About the argument, never the person.** No gloating that others agreed, no
  guessing why they went quiet, no "your video" when it wasn't theirs. "The
  last take," not "your take."
- **Match their precision.** Use their words for their claims. Don't loosen
  "reverse engineering" into "reading code."
- **Don't repeat what's already been sent.** A follow-up builds on the last
  message, it doesn't re-say it.
- **Name the pattern, not the person.** When pivots have started, recapture the
  frame once ("each of these freezes one side and extrapolates the other")
  instead of answering anecdote by anecdote.
- **No forecasts and no side debates.** Argue from structure. Leave out
  anything unverified.
- **Short.** If it doesn't fit on a phone screen or two, cut it.
