**Art is a selective re-creation of reality according to an artist's metaphysical value-judgments — Objectivism's account of its fifth branch, included here for completeness.**

## What It Answers

[[Philosophy|The chain]] gets to [[Ethics]]: a rational being needs values to guide choice and action. But values, as Ethics states them, are abstractions — "rationality," "productiveness," "man qua man" — held in conceptual form, not directly perceived. Aesthetics asks: how does a being who thinks conceptually ever get to *see* his own philosophy, all at once, the way he sees a chair or a face?

## The Psycho-Epistemological Function of Art

Human cognition works by abstraction — concepts subsuming concepts, most of them held as compressed symbols rather than vivid percepts. The widest abstractions of all — one's view of what kind of universe this is, what man is, what's possible to him — are the most compressed and the hardest to hold in focus at once. Rand's argument: man needs a way to grasp these ultimate abstractions *as if* they were percepts — concrete, delimited, graspable in a single act of awareness — or they stay inert, unable to actually guide him moment to moment.

Art is that concretization. A novel, a painting, a symphony takes an artist's widest view of existence and re-creates it in a form small enough and vivid enough to be held in one act of consciousness — the way a single object is held in one act of perception. This is why Rand treats art as a psycho-epistemological need, not a decorative one: it's what lets a conceptual consciousness actually *use* its own philosophy.

## Sense of Life

The companion concept is **sense of life**: "a pre-conceptual equivalent of metaphysics, an emotional, subconsciously integrated appraisal of man and of existence." Long before anyone states their metaphysics explicitly, they've already formed one — an implicit, felt sense of whether existence is hospitable or hostile, whether man is competent or doomed, built out of a lifetime of small, mostly-never-examined value-integrations.

Art is what makes sense of life visible, to its own creator and to an audience. Two people can read the same novel and one feels at home in it while the other recoils — that's not a disagreement about craft, it's two senses of life meeting the same concretized worldview and either recognizing themselves in it or not.

## What Counts as Art

For Rand, art has to be a **selective, perceptually-graspable re-creation** — two requirements doing two different jobs. *Selective* rules out mere reportage: a work that records everything indiscriminately (or nothing in particular) isn't re-creating a view of what matters, it's failing to have one. *Perceptually graspable* rules out the purely theoretical: philosophy stated as bare propositions isn't yet art, because propositions still have to be held in conceptual form — art's specific job is to make the abstraction concrete enough to perceive. A story, a representational painting, an organized piece of music qualify; random paint or noise doesn't — not because it's ugly, but because no selection is being made, so nothing is being said.

## Romantic Realism vs. Naturalism

Rand's own school — **Romantic Realism** — is built on a single fork: does man have volition? **Naturalism** says no; man is a product of heredity, environment, and circumstance, and art's job is to report him "as he was," deterministically. **Romanticism** says yes; man is a being of self-made character who chooses, and art's job is to show him choosing — which is why Rand valued plot (purposeful action toward a goal) over mood or reportage: plot is the structure that actually dramatizes volition instead of just describing its absence. Her own novels — *The Fountainhead*, *Atlas Shrugged* — are her own practice of this: heroes defined by chosen values, pursued against resistance, not people things merely happen to.

## Where This Sits in the Chain

Peikoff's own schema is explicit: **Metaphysics and Epistemology → Ethics → Art.** Aesthetics isn't derived straight from Metaphysics the way Politics was wrongly derived straight from a claim about "objective law" — it comes after Ethics, because *selecting* what's essential (the core of the definition of art) is itself a value judgment, and values are Ethics's department. ## Why This One Doesn't Need Correcting

Unlike Politics, nothing in this branch smuggles in an institution. Aesthetics makes no claim about force, government, or law — it's a claim about cognition and value, full stop. Whatever disagreements exist about it (Rand and Peikoff privilege literature, specifically plotted narrative, as the paradigm case of art, on the grounds that only a time-based, choice-driven medium can dramatize volition directly — a claim even sympathetic critics push back on, since music and architecture arguably perform the same concretizing function without a plot) are internal, aesthetic disagreements, not points where the [[The Non-aggression Principle|NAP]] is at stake. There's nothing here for [[Anarcho-Capitalism|anarcho-capitalism]] to fix, which is exactly why this vault's critique of Objectivism stops at Politics.
