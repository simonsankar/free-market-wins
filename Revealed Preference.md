---
title: Revealed Preference
date: 2026-07-30
---

> A person's actual choices — not their stated beliefs or intentions — are the most reliable evidence of what they truly value. Formalized by economist Paul Samuelson in 1938 as a neoclassical demand-theory tool. The closer Austrian-school analogue is Murray Rothbard's **demonstrated preference**, developed to argue, in line with [[Austrian Economics|praxeology]], that only observed action — not verbal claims — can establish someone's true preferences.

## Action Speaks, Words Don't

Every [[Human action|action]] is a choice of one thing over another. When you buy coffee for three dollars, you've shown that — at that moment — you valued the coffee more than the three dollars. Nobody needs to ask you. The act is the answer.

Words are different. Saying costs nothing. A person can say he values the environment while flying twice a month, say he supports local shops while buying online, say he wants higher taxes while claiming every deduction. None of those statements is a lie, exactly. They're just not choices. When saying and doing diverge, the doing is the preference; the saying is how the person would like to be seen.

Rothbard's version is stricter than Samuelson's. Samuelson treated choices as data for building a stable utility function. Rothbard denied that any such function can be inferred: action reveals what was preferred *at that moment*, between *those options*, and nothing more ("Toward a Reconstruction of Utility and Welfare Economics", 1956). Value is [[Subjective Value Theory|subjective]] and can't be measured or added up — but it can be shown, one act at a time.

## What It Proves About Voluntary Exchange

In a voluntary [[Trade|trade]], each party gives up something he values less for something he values more. Both have demonstrated that they expected to gain — otherwise neither would have traded. So every voluntary exchange is, by demonstrated preference, mutually beneficial in the eyes of the only people who can judge.

Coerced "exchange" proves the opposite. If a person had to be forced to hand over his money, he has demonstrated that he preferred to keep it. [[Taxation]] can't be shown to benefit the taxpayer, because the one act that would show it — paying voluntarily — is exactly what the tax prevents.

## Using It

Revealed preference cuts through three kinds of claim:

- **Polls vs behaviour.** "71% oppose data centres near them" is a statement. Whether the same people use AI tools, stream video and store their photos in the cloud — all of which run in data centres — is the demonstration.
- **Stated vs chosen.** The socialist who picks the higher-paying private job, the critic of the free market who shops for the lowest price, the advocate of public schools who sends his own children private — each has shown which system he trusts when it's his own money on the line.
- **"People really want X."** If they wanted it, they'd buy it. When the claim is that people want something but must be *made* to have it, the demonstrated preference is the opposite of the claim.
