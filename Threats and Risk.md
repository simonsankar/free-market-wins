A threat is an act aimed at a victim. A risk is a property of the world. Self-defence answers the first. Nothing licenses force against the second.

## When a Threat Is Already Aggression

You don't have to wait to be shot. A man pointing a gun at you, a mugger with a raised knife, a written demand "pay or I burn your shop" — each is already [[Aggression]]. The aggressor has begun an act directed at a specific victim, and the victim may defend himself now.

What makes it a threat, and not merely a risk:

- **A specific aggressor** — a person, not a category of people or a technology.
- **A specific victim** — you, your property, not "society".
- **An overt act** — something done, not something possible. A raised knife, not a knife in a drawer.
- **Imminence** — the invasion is under way or about to be, not a forecast.

Take any of those away and there is nobody to defend against.

## Why Risk Isn't Aggression

Everything is risky. Driving, cooking with gas, owning a pool, keeping a dog, running a lab, writing software, teaching chemistry. Each raises the chance that someone, someday, gets hurt. If creating risk were aggression, every action would be aggression, and the [[The Non-aggression Principle|NAP]] would forbid life itself — which is how you know that reading is wrong.

Risk is handled the way all other uncertainty is handled: by the people who bear it, voluntarily. Insurers price it and demand safety conditions before they'll cover you. Landlords and customers write it into [[Contracts]]. And when a risk does turn into a real invasion, [[Liability and Restitution]] makes the person who caused it pay. Actors who face the bill take care, because they have [[Skin in the game]].

## Prior Restraint

Prior restraint is force used against people who have harmed no one, because someone fears what they *might* do. Bans, moratoria, licences, and possession charges all work this way.

Its fatal property is that it restrains the wrong people. The man already willing to commit murder isn't deterred by a weapons charge, a licensing rule, or a ban — he has already decided to break a far graver law. The people who comply are the ones who were never going to harm anyone. Ban guns and you disarm the shooter's victims, not the shooter. License a technology and you bind the honest labs, not the black market and not the state.

And it hands the decision to whoever fears most loudly. "Too dangerous" has no threshold, so a prior-restraint regime is one group deciding, by its own estimate, what everyone else may do.

## The Special Case

The standard escape is: *principles are fine normally, but this one is different — too catastrophic, too irreversible, too new.* Extinction, pandemics, superintelligence, bioweapons.

There is no such case. Every argument for intervention takes this form, because every intervention is a group introducing conflict on the grounds that it knows what's best for society. Raising the stakes changes the rhetoric, not the logic. Ask for the threshold — how catastrophic is catastrophic enough, and derived from what? — and there's no answer, only whoever is in charge deciding. [[The Alien Ultimatum - Why Parasitic Ethics Cannot Answer Their Own Hypotheticals|The Alien Ultimatum]] makes the point at its limit: not even a penny of aggression to save humanity, because once a penny is admissible, so is everything else, at a price set by whim.

Note also who the catastrophic risks have actually been. Governments killed somewhere between 169 and over 260 million of their own people in the 20th century, outside of war (R. J. Rummel's democide counts). The USSR signed the Biological Weapons Convention in 1972 and founded Biopreparat, the largest offensive bioweapons programme ever run, two years later. A prior-restraint regime over dangerous capabilities puts the proven killer in charge of the gate.
