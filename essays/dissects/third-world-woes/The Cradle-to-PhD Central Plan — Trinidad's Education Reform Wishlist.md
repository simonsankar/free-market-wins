## A 28-Point Reddit Proposal to Nationalize Every Trinidadian From Daycare to PhD

**The Stated Problem**

A widely-shared r/trinidad post proposes 28 "doable steps" to reform child and tertiary education — free daycare through PhD, state-built "gymnasiums" that sort 11-year-olds into castes, a state-run "Young Pioneers" talent-scouting program, imported foreign experts on state-subsidized housing, GATE dissolved and replaced with unconditional free tertiary education, and a state-directed industrial policy funneling graduates into "diversified" state-selected industries — all in pursuit of turning Trinidad into "a first world nation."

**The Shallow Take**

The fix, in the author's telling, is simply *more government, better organized*. More subsidies, more free things, more state-built institutions, more imported bureaucratic expertise (Finnish teacher trainers, Chinese life-skills coaches, Israeli Talpiot consultants), more central coordination between ministries, universities, and industry. Every single failure point in Trinidad's current system is diagnosed as *insufficient state investment* rather than *state involvement itself*. Not one of the 28 points asks whether the Ministry of Education should be doing any of this at all — only whether it's doing enough of it.

**The Actual Sin(s)**

**1. NAP Violation — All the Way Down**

Nearly every proposal is stamped "state subsidized," "wholly free," "state covered," or "state backed." That language always means the same thing: some portion of what a productive Trinidadian earns will be seized under threat of imprisonment and redirected toward daycare, tablets, gymnasium architecture, imported Finnish consultants, and subsidized homes for imported engineers. There is no version of "free" university, "free" tablets for every child standard-4-to-form-5, and "free housing" for imported experts that doesn't route through [[Taxation]] — coercive extraction from people who never agreed to fund any of it. The entire 28-point plan is a monument to the Consent Fiction: the word "free" is doing the work of hiding "extracted from someone else at gunpoint."

**2. Economic Calculation Problem — An Entire Industrial Policy With No Prices**

The tertiary section's proposals for "institutions of research laboratories, factories, business companies... established to put intellectuals to work and diversify the economy" aren't education policy — they're central industrial planning. Which industries? Medicine, computers, AI, software, electronics — chosen not because market prices signaled unmet demand and profit opportunity, but because a Reddit poster decided these sound like first-world industries. This is exactly what the [[Economic Calculation Problem]] says is impossible: without market prices for capital goods, there is no way to know whether a state biotech lab is a better use of resources than the thousand things it wasn't spent on. The state cannot "diversify the economy" by decree — it can only misallocate capital into ventures no one paid for with their own money.

**3. Knowledge Problem — The Gymnasium Sorting Hat**

The plan proposes testing every 11-year-old in the country to identify "the most academically gifted" and funnel them into elite state schools, with results "not publicized" to manage psychological optics. This assumes a central authority can identify, at age 11, which children will be most valuable to society decades later — a claim the [[Knowledge Problem (Information Throughput Problem)]] demolishes outright. Human potential is not a fixed, testable quantity a bureaucrat can extract with a standardized exam at age 11; it's discovered over a lifetime through decentralized trial, error, and market feedback. No psychologist-administered test in a state "Young Pioneers" program can replace the dispersed, tacit information generated by millions of people making their own choices about what to study and pursue. This is the Soviet "talent identification" model, dressed in Trinidad flag colors.

**4. Incentive Inversion — Handing the Arsonist a Bigger Match**

[[Ministry of Education]] already has a documented track record: $35,000 USD/month on cloud infrastructure serving essentially zero users, a bureaucratic segmented dysfunctional mess with no incentive structure connecting effort to outcome. This 28-point plan proposes giving that exact institution control over every child from birth to PhD, a national talent-scouting apparatus, a dedicated science-and-engineering university, multiple new gymnasium campuses, and an industrial policy portfolio. In government, failure doesn't shrink the budget — it justifies a bigger one next year. This isn't a plan to fix the ministry. It's a plan to 10x its blast radius.

**5. The GATE-Bond Trap, Rebuilt and Made Worse**

The proposal that graduates accepting state-covered education "must work 3 years in Trinidad before going abroad" is not new. It is the exact mechanism already dissected in [[The Academic Achievement Trap - Trinidad Scholarship Edition]]: government-funded credentials with mandatory local-service strings attached, which trap the most capable graduates in low-value government placements during precisely the years their human capital is most valuable, while their skills depreciate and market-experienced peers lap them. That essay documents this isn't hypothetical — it's the observed, current outcome of Trinidad's scholarship-bond system. This post proposes scaling that exact failure mode to every single tertiary graduate in the country, not just scholarship winners.

**6. Monopoly Protection — State as Sole Educator, Sole Employer, Sole Central Planner**

Community colleges "built across the land," a dedicated state science university, state-run gymnasiums, state industrial labs — this is a wall-to-wall state monopoly over the entire pipeline from cradle to career. No competitive pressure, no exit option, no price discovery about which schools, curricula, or industries actually serve people well. [[The Myth of Limited Government]] applies directly: once granted this scope, this ministry — like every government before it — will not spontaneously self-limit. It will expand.

**7. Cantillon Effect — Subsidized Housing for the Politically Selected**

The plan proposes "specially built neighbourhoods," free housing, subsidized land, and vehicles for imported and favored local experts — funded by new spending that must come from taxation or money creation. Per the [[Cantillon Effect]], this money doesn't appear equally for everyone; it flows first to the politically connected (foreign hires, construction contractors, gymnasium architects) who spend it at old prices before the rest of the economy adjusts. Meanwhile, the productive Trinidadians funding all of this get the bill, not the subsidized housing.

**Market Proof**

Every "free education" goal in this post already exists — right now, at zero tax dollars, without a single gymnasium or ministry hire. Khan Academy, Coursera, and freeCodeCamp deliver university-caliber instruction in math, science, and computer science to anyone on Earth with a phone, for free, funded voluntarily by donors and paying customers who value the platform enough to subsidize free tiers. Duolingo turned language acquisition into something hundreds of millions of people do for fun. No 11-year-olds were tested and sorted to build any of this. No ministry spent $35,000/month on cloud infrastructure serving zero users to make it happen.

Compare that to what this post proposes: an entire government apparatus — new campuses, new bureaucratic layers, imported consultants housed in specially built neighbourhoods, a national gifted-child screening program — to deliver something the internet already solved for the cost of a WiFi connection. Silicon Valley recruits the best engineers on Earth without a single subsidized neighborhood or state housing program — it just pays market wages, and talent shows up. Trinidad doesn't need a Ministry-run Talpiot. It needs to stop taxing the entrepreneurs who'd build the local equivalent of Khan Academy if they were allowed to keep what they earn.

**The Verdict**

Every one of these 28 proposals fails not because of poor execution, corrupt ministers, or insufficient funding — it fails because it's built on the premise that a handful of central planners can price, allocate, and predict outcomes for an entire nation's human capital better than millions of individuals making voluntary choices with their own resources. That premise is not just wrong, it's logically impossible, as the Economic Calculation Problem and Knowledge Problem prove independently of any particular government's competence. And underneath the calculation problem sits the deeper violation: none of it can be funded without first taking, by force, from people who never agreed to pay for a gymnasium, a subsidized neighborhood for imported scientists, or a national talent-scouting program for other people's children.

The actual path to "first world" status isn't a bigger, better-funded planning apparatus — it's fewer obstacles between Trinidadians and the value they're already capable of creating voluntarily. Stop taxing capital formation. Stop licensing and monopolizing schooling. Let price signals and voluntary exchange do what no ministry, however well-intentioned, is structurally capable of doing. The nations that got rich didn't get there by testing 11-year-olds — they got there by leaving people alone.
