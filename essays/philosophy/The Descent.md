title: The Descent
date: 2026-10-10

**Every argument for intervention can be followed downward, one question at a time, until it either concedes or hits bedrock. This is the ladder it goes down. Each rung answers the one above it, and each is a place where the other side can reasonably say "fine, you're right" and stop.**

Most disagreements never get far. "Ban crack, it wrecks lives" ends at the top, on a value both sides share. A position only reaches the deep rungs when its holder refuses every exit on the way down — which is what [[The Selective Guillotine]] traces in one long conversation. A rebuttal that settles a claim at a shallow rung isn't weaker than one that goes to the floor. It's cheaper. Use the shallowest rung that works, and know what's beneath it in case the exit is refused.

Dissects mark every rebuttal with the rung it bottoms out at, written as *Economics: Scarcity → Rights*: answered at the first, with the second underneath if that exit is refused. Each one links back here.

## Policy

- **The question:** what is actually being done?
- **The refusal:** "It's just a contribution." "It's a safety net." "It's a guardrail."
- **The answer:** name the mechanism. A tax is taking without consent; a ban is force against people who've harmed no one; a licence is permission you need from someone with no right to give it.
- **Notes:** [[Taxation]], [[Aggression]]

## Economics

- **The question:** can it work?
- **The refusal:** "Government will do it better." "Something must be done."

Most topical fights — trade, housing, healthcare, technology — happen here, so this rung has four parts.

### Incentives

- **The question:** who pays for being wrong?
- **The answer:** a private actor who errs loses his own money. An official who errs loses nothing, and often gains budget. The institution with no skin in the game will be wrong in a predictable direction.
- **Notes:** [[Skin in the game]], [[Inverse incentive structure]], [[Regulatory Capture]]

### Knowledge

- **The question:** who has the information, and how does it travel?
- **The answer:** prices carry what millions of people know about their own wants and circumstances. Remove them and no planner, however clever, can know what to produce.
- **Notes:** [[economic calculation problem]], [[Knowledge Problem (Information Throughput Problem)]], [[price system]]

### Scarcity

- **The question:** what is given up, and where does the scarcity move?
- **The answer:** every resource used one way isn't used another. A benefit counted without its cost is half an argument, and making one input cheap doesn't end scarcity, it moves it to the next want.
- **Notes:** [[Scarcity]], [[Seen and Unseen]], [[Lump of Labour]], [[Production]]

### Action

- **The question:** who acts, toward what end, and do they respond to change?
- **The answer:** people always act to improve their situation, so any argument that needs them to stand still while one thing changes contradicts the [[Axiom of Action]] — a stronger result than "that seems unlikely". This is where economics touches the floor.
- **Notes:** [[Human action]], [[Subjective Value Theory]], [[Revealed Preference]]

## Authority

- **The question:** who gets to decide for others?
- **The refusal:** "We voted for it." "The experts agree." "It's an emergency."
- **The answer:** a majority can't hand over a right none of its voters have, an expert's knowledge isn't a right to rule, and an emergency doesn't change who owns what. There is no situation special enough to license aggression — not even a penny to save humanity ([[The Alien Ultimatum - Why Parasitic Ethics Cannot Answer Their Own Hypotheticals|The Alien Ultimatum]]).
- **Notes:** [[Democracy]], [[Jungle Law]], [[Threats and Risk]]

## Rights

- **The question:** whose is it, and was it invaded?
- **The refusal:** "It's society's money." "His use of his land affects me." "He's made my work worth less."
- **The answer:** you own your body and what you homesteaded, produced or traded for. You own its physical integrity, not its market value. Only a physical invasion is aggression, and its remedy is restitution from the person who did it.
- **Notes:** [[Self-ownership]], [[private property]], [[Homesteading (First-comer Ethic)]], [[Physical Integrity vs Value]], [[Liability and Restitution]], [[Conflicts]]

## Equality

- **The question:** does the same rule bind everyone?
- **The refusal:** "Taking is fine when the state does it." "Rulers and ruled are different."
- **The answer:** if men are equal, a law that lets some take from others can't follow. If they aren't, who exactly is sub-human? One rule for rulers and another for you is [[Mixed Law]].
- **Notes:** [[Mixed Law]], [[State]], [[The Non-aggression Principle]]

## Logic

- **The question:** is it consistent?
- **The refusal:** "Different logic applies to different people." "A can be non-A when I need it to be."
- **The answer:** arguing for many logics uses exactly one. And the law of identity is the floor: every argument, including this refusal, already relies on it.
- **Notes:** [[Polylogism]], [[Argumentation Ethics]], [[Law of Non-Contradiction]], [[Identity]]

## Below the floor

The last move left is to say that none of it binds anyone — *you can't derive an ought from an is*, and contradictions don't matter. That isn't a counter-argument. It's a refusal to make one, and a man who has made it has left the conversation, not won it. See "The Line in the Sand" in [[The Selective Guillotine]].
