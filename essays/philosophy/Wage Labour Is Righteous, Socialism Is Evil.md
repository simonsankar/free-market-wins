---
title: Wage Labour Is Righteous, Socialism Is Evil
date: 2023-10-06
---

Both halves of this come down to the same test: was force used, or wasn't it? Apply it consistently and wage labour and socialism land on opposite sides.

## Wage Labour Is Righteous

The Marxist claim is that workers don't get their "fair share" under capitalism because profits go to the capitalist. This is a straight-up entitlement claim on someone else's property with no basis:

- The capitalist forwent consumption to accumulate the capital required to start the business.
- The worker is paid an agreed-upon wage, even before they're productive in any meaningful capacity, and regardless of whether profits ever come in.
- The capitalist alone bears the risk. If things go south, does the worker want loss-sharing too — i.e., working for less or nothing?

Once again, this is really about abolishing [[Private Property]], which is aggression against an individual — and we know aggression to be false through [[The Non-aggression Principle]], backed by [[Argumentation Ethics]] and underpinned by the [[Primacy of Existence]].

## Socialism Is Evil

[[Socialism]] necessitates the violation of individuals' self-sovereignty. A portion of their private property is taken from them to fund ventures they never consented to. The individual is reduced to a tool for someone else's benefit — percentage-based slavery, since they don't keep all of what they earn.

Because it requires [[Aggression]], we know socialism to be false.

## Same Axiom, Opposite Verdicts

Wage labour is a voluntary trade: both parties agree, both can walk away, no one is forced to pay a cost they didn't accept. Socialism is the opposite: one party's gain is manufactured by force against another party who never agreed to fund it. The ethics aren't a matter of which party you sympathize with — they're a matter of whether consent was present. It was, in one case. It wasn't, in the other.
