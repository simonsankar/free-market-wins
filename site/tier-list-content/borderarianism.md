The claim: open borders only work as policy if there's no welfare state waiting on the other side — combine free movement with subsidized housing, healthcare, and benefits, and you get people crossing borders specifically to collect a check nobody's crossing to earn. An ancap who supports open borders while a welfare state still exists is subsidizing exactly the free-riding they claim to oppose.

That's a real problem with the *current, mixed system* — a state border paired with a state welfare program — not with anarcho-capitalism, which has neither. There's no public land to cross without anyone's permission and no taxpayer-funded benefit office to collect from, because every road, every neighborhood, and every service is [[private property|someone's actual property]]: movement is a matter of whoever owns the land you're standing on consenting to your being there, the same as it is for anyone already living there. "Free-riding" requires a free ride to exist in the first place, and a fully privatized order simply doesn't have one — access is granted by owners who bear the cost of the decision, not extracted by newcomers from a pool everyone's forced to fund. If anything, the objection is an argument for finishing the privatization, not an argument against it: the free-riding only exists because the border and the welfare state are both still government programs.
