The claim: even if you start from a genuinely stateless market of competing defense and legal agencies, competitive pressure and consolidation will eventually produce a single dominant agency — at which point you've just re-invented a state under a different name, and the whole system was unstable from the start.

The consolidation story skips over why states actually manage to stay consolidated once formed: a monopoly on legitimate force, a captive tax base that funds itself regardless of performance, and a legal system that pre-declares its own actions lawful. A defense agency that "wins" market share in a stateless order has none of those — it has paying customers who can fire it, no money-printer, and rivals who profit precisely by picking off its dissatisfied clients, so growing bigger increases its costs and its exposure to competitors faster than it increases its power. [[Anarcho-Capitalism deters state formation]] walks through exactly this mechanism: buying out or crushing your competition costs real resources that then sit in the hands of whoever you paid, strengthening your future rivals with every "win." A single firm becoming dominant is theoretically possible the way any firm cornering any market is theoretically possible — but "dominant market share" and "the monopoly on legitimate violence funded by compulsory taxation" are different things, and only a very specific, expensive, and self-defeating path connects one to the other.
