The claim: the entire ancap framework runs on self-ownership and voluntary contract between competent adults, and children are neither — they can't consent, can't exit a bad household, can't meaningfully contract, and can't be left to "homestead" their own survival. If self-ownership and contract are the only two ways rights and obligations arise, the theory has no native account of parents' obligations to children or children's rights against parents at all.

This is the honest edge case, and it deserves more than a slogan. The way it actually resolves: [[Self-ownership]] isn't granted at a moment, it's a fact about being the entity that directs a given mind and body — a child already is that entity, just not yet capable of exercising the full judgment self-ownership assumes. A parent who creates a fully dependent, non-self-sufficient person doesn't thereby acquire ownership *of* them; they acquire an obligation *to* them, a trustee relationship over a self-owner who can't yet exercise their own claim — and severe neglect or abandonment of that trust is itself an act of aggression against the child's person, actionable the same as aggression against anyone else, not a private family matter exempt from the [[The Non-aggression Principle|NAP]]. What the framework doesn't hand you for free is a clean bright line for exactly when a child's say-so starts binding and exactly what threshold of care a guardian owes before "custody" shades into "aggression" — that line gets drawn the way every hard case in a legal system gets drawn, case by case, rather than deduced in one step from the axioms. That's a genuine gap in the theory's tidiness, not a hole in its underlying ethics.
