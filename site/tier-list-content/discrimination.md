The claim: without civil rights law forcing businesses to serve everyone, private discrimination against protected groups would run rampant — nothing stops a business from freely refusing service based on race, religion, or any other prejudice.

It's your property — you decide who you do business with, for any reason or none, the same way you decide who you invite into your home. [[private property|Ownership means the exclusive right to direct a resource's use]], and that includes the resource of your own labor and capital; forcing a transaction on an unwilling party is a use of force against their property regardless of how sympathetic the reason for the mandate sounds. The market punishes irrational discrimination by leaving money on the table — a bigoted business turns away paying customers and leaves a gap for a competitor who'll happily take their money, which is exactly why segregation-era businesses needed *state-enforced* segregation laws to hold the line against the market's own pressure to integrate for profit. That's the actual check on irrational discrimination: competitors bidding for the customers you turned away, not a law — and it doesn't require anyone to prove their reasons were good ones first.
