The claim: some goods are non-excludable and non-rivalrous — once produced, anyone can benefit whether they paid or not — so private producers under-supply them and the state has to step in and fund them through taxation.

There's no coherent definition of "public good" on offer here that isn't just "thing I want subsidized." Real-world producers solve the free-rider problem constantly without a state: bundling (ad-supported content, HOA-maintained private roads, business-district lighting funded by the merchants who benefit), exclusion technology (paywalls, encryption, fencing), and reputation/dominant-assurance contracts that only execute once enough people commit. Lighthouses, famously cited by economists as the textbook unownable public good, were privately built and funded in Britain via harbor-due contracts before the state nationalized the industry. Where genuine non-excludability persists, the market still allocates the resulting scarcity better and cheaper than a taxing authority with no price signal and no profit-and-loss discipline ever can — see [[Economic Calculation Problem]]. The "market failure" here isn't a failure to produce the good; it's a failure to produce it *for free*, which was never actually on offer from the state either — it just moved the bill to a coerced payer instead of a voluntary one.
