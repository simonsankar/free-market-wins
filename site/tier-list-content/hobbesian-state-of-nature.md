The claim: humans are brutish and self-interested by nature, so absent a sovereign holding a monopoly on force to keep everyone in check, life reverts to a war of all against all — anarchy isn't a peaceful default, it's the thing government rescues us from.

Hobbes's own solution doesn't escape the problem, it just relocates it: if men are too brutish to be trusted without an all-powerful enforcer above them, why would the specific men who become that enforcer suddenly become trustworthy the moment they hold total, unaccountable power over everyone else? You haven't left the state of nature, you've just declared one party inside it the winner and given them a monopoly on the very violence you were worried about. [[State|A state is that monopoly]], not an escape from "might makes right" — it's might makes right with better branding. And the "war of all against all" framing already fails on its own terms the moment you're arguing about it with someone: describing a shared hypothetical, appealing to their reason, expecting them to weigh your claim — all of that is cooperative, conflict-free behavior that [[Argumentation Ethics|presupposes the very non-aggression norm]] the Hobbesian picture says can't exist without a sovereign forcing it into being.
