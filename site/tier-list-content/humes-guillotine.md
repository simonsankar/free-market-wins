The claim: you can't derive an "ought" from an "is" — no amount of description of self-ownership, scarcity, or human nature can logically entail a moral conclusion like "aggression is wrong," so any rights framework built on Austrian premises is smuggling in a value judgment it never actually proved.

Self-defeating when used as a moral argument — "you can't derive an ought from an is" is itself a normative claim about how arguments should work, smuggling in the same move it forbids: it tells you what conclusions you *ought* to accept as validly derived, which is exactly the ought-from-is inference it's declaring illegitimate. [[Argumentation Ethics]] doesn't try to cross the gap by describing human nature and then tacking a moral conclusion onto the end anyway — it shows that the "ought" is already presupposed the moment anyone engages in argumentation at all, since arguing is a conflict-free act that requires exclusive control over your own mind and body to perform. You cannot argue against non-aggression without already relying on it, which means the norm isn't derived from a neutral "is" — it's a condition of the very activity of arguing for or against anything, guillotine included.
