The claim: you can't derive an "ought" from an "is" — no amount of description of self-ownership, scarcity, or human nature can logically entail a moral conclusion like "aggression is wrong," so any rights framework built on Austrian premises is smuggling in a value judgment it never actually proved.

Grant the logic — it's true that no purely descriptive premises validly entail a normative conclusion. But "that inference is invalid" is itself just a fact, and by Hume's own rule a fact alone can't tell anyone what to do. To turn it into "so abandon your ethics," the skeptic needs an extra premise: *one ought not believe what isn't validly derived.* That's an epistemic "ought," and no "is" produces it either. So he's stuck. If that norm genuinely binds, then underived oughts can bind everyone, and "your ethics rests on an ought you didn't derive from an is" stops being an objection — his rests on one too. If it doesn't bind, then invalidity carries no force against anybody, and he can't call any belief wrong, the NAP included; the guillotine shrinks to "I don't feel like agreeing." Either way, the blade can't be swung at libertarian ethics. [[Argumentation Ethics]] then supplies the positive case: non-aggression is presupposed right alongside that same epistemic norm the moment anyone argues at all. And watch where the guillotine actually gets used — never against "billionaires ought to pay their fair share," only against the side whose oughts have foundations, and only after the positive case for the state has already collapsed.
