The claim: private defense and protection firms are, functionally, private military companies — and history shows PMCs tend to go rogue, switch sides, or become armed factions once they realize they hold the guns.

Every historical PMC that "went rogue" did so in a context with a state nearby to seize, bribe, or become — the profitable move for an armed group has always been capturing a tax base and legal monopoly, not competing indefinitely for voluntary customers who can fire you. Take the state off the table as an achievable prize and the incentive changes completely: a defense firm that turns on its own paying clients loses the reputation and insurer/arbitrator relationships that make it viable at all, the same market discipline described in [[Anarcho-Capitalism deters state formation|why a would-be aggressor in a stateless order has to buy market share instead of seizing it]] — every customer it terrorizes is a customer for a rival firm, funded by the very people it just alienated. Compare the actual track record: mercenary abuses documented under state contracts, where the customer is a government with no exit option and no market discipline of its own, against a hypothetical stateless market that hasn't existed yet to accumulate that record. The objection borrows its evidence from exactly the system it's trying to defend.
