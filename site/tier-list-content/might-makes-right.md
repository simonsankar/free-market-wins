The claim: without a state monopolizing force, there's no referee, so whoever has the most guns just rules everyone else — anarchy collapses into raw power.

This gets the direction backwards. A [[State]] *is* might-makes-right — it's the one entity that has already won the fight and made its victory permanent, backed by police, prisons, and a money-printer to fund all three. Removing it doesn't introduce a power struggle, it removes the institution that already settled one by force and locked in the result. What replaces it isn't a vacuum, it's competing [[Contracts|contract]]-based defense agencies who answer to paying customers instead of conscripts, checked by each other the same way any market actor is checked by competitors. And ethically the claim never had legs to begin with: "the strong decide" is [[Jungle Law]], one of only two alternatives to the [[The Non-aggression Principle|NAP]], and both collapse into the [[Primacy of Consciousness]] fallacy — reality doesn't bend because someone hit hardest, and [[Argumentation Ethics]] shows you can't even argue for that position without contradicting it.
