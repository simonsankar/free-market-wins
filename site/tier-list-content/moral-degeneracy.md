The claim: capitalism makes people greedy, atomized, and culturally hollow — markets optimize for consumption and self-interest at the expense of virtue and community.

This is a complaint about *culture*, smuggled in as a complaint about *property rights*. Markets don't mandate greed any more than free speech mandates lies — they're a mechanism for peaceful exchange that's silent on what you value or why. People are free to build tight-knit communities, give generously, live ascetically, or found monasteries inside a market order; nothing about voluntary exchange forbids virtue, and plenty of the "atomization" blamed on markets is actually downstream of the state hollowing out the family, church, and mutual-aid networks it displaced with bureaucratic substitutes. And the tell is that this critique is never aimed at the specific transaction that supposedly caused the decay — it's aimed at capitalism in the abstract, which means it isn't really an argument against markets, it's an aesthetic preference dressed up as one, and aesthetic preferences don't license using force to rearrange other people's property to match your taste in culture.
