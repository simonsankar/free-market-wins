The claim: wilderness, reserves, and iconic landscapes need to be preserved by government, because no profit-seeking owner would leave land undeveloped for its own sake.

Land trusts, private nature reserves, and conservation easements already exist and already do exactly this — owners restrict or forgo development on their own property all the time, funded by donors, ecotourism revenue, or the owner's own preference for holding an asset unspoiled rather than monetized, the same way a collector holds art rather than selling it. [[private property|Property rights]] don't require the owner to extract maximum short-term value; they just require someone to have the exclusive right to decide, and plenty of owners decide "preserved." What state-run parks actually deliver is a resource nobody in particular is accountable for maintaining, funded by taxpayers who never chose that specific park over some other use of their money and staffed by an agency with no [[price system|price signal]] telling it whether it's managing the land well or catastrophically — see the wildfire-fuel-buildup and infrastructure-backlog record of most public park systems for how well "nobody owns it" actually protects wilderness.
