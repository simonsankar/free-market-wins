The claim: some markets — courts, roads, protection, utilities — have such large economies of scale that competition is unsustainable; whoever gets big first crushes every rival and you end up with one provider anyway, so you might as well make it accountable and call it government.

Doesn't happen absent state intervention — every real "natural monopoly" in history was created or protected by state-granted privilege (franchises, licensing, regulatory capture), not by market forces alone. The textbook examples — railroads, utilities, telecoms — all became single-provider markets *because* a government granted an exclusive franchise or erected licensing barriers that made a second entrant illegal or prohibitively expensive to certify, not because economies of scale made competition physically impossible. Real markets with genuine scale advantages still see entrants chip away at incumbents through niches, better service, or new technology the incumbent has no incentive to adopt once competition is banned. A monopoly that has to keep winning every customer's business every day, with no legal barrier stopping a rival from trying, is a fundamentally different animal from a monopoly that got its position from a government permit — and it's only the second kind that ever actually shows up.
