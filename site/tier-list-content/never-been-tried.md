The claim: no stateless-market society has ever existed at scale, so it's a utopian thought experiment, not a serious proposal — and the handful of historical counterexamples (medieval Iceland, etc.) always come with an asterisk.

"Nobody's tried it" is an appeal to tradition, and appeals to tradition don't establish anything about whether a thing is right — slavery, absolute monarchy, and rule by divine right all had much longer track records than liberal democracy did when it was the untested new idea. Ethics isn't settled by a historical popularity contest. But the deeper problem with the objection is that it treats the absence of the alternative as neutral evidence, when it's manufactured: every existing state holds a monopoly on force *within its own borders* and uses it to criminalize competing legal and defense arrangements — the thing being asked to occur "naturally" is illegal everywhere it might start. That's not evidence against anarcho-capitalism, it's evidence of exactly what a state monopoly is built to prevent from ever getting off the ground. See [[The Systematic Failure of Statist Arguments]] for the fuller version of this pattern — the same "why hasn't it happened" question, asked about literally any other suppressed innovation, was never actually asked in good faith.
