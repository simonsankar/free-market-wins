The claim: without welfare programs, poor people simply starve or go without medical care — the state is the backstop that catches people the market has no incentive to catch.

A freed market makes goods cheaper and money sound, which does more for the poor than any welfare program — every layer of licensing, tariffs, and [[Inflation]]-driven currency debasement stripped away is a direct cut to the cost of food, housing, and medicine for people with the least slack to absorb higher prices. That doesn't erase personal responsibility for the subset of poverty that's circumstance-independent, and it doesn't need to: charity still exists in this world, but it's freely given rather than extracted, so it comes with real social accountability — shame, gratitude, reputational stakes — instead of being an unconditional entitlement that a bureaucracy hands out regardless of effort or need. [[Parasitism|The consume-before-produce ethic]] that welfare formalizes doesn't disappear when you fund it through taxation instead of asking nicely; it just adds a monopoly middleman who takes a cut and has no market feedback telling him he's doing it badly.
