The claim: a right to healthcare or education is as real and as morally binding as a right to not be assaulted or robbed — both are "rights," the only difference is what they require from society to fulfill.

They fail a test the other passes: can everyone hold the right simultaneously with zero conflict? Negative rights — don't hit me, don't take my stuff — can be honored by every person on Earth at once, since honoring them requires nothing but abstaining from action. A "right" to healthcare requires a specific doctor's specific labor on a specific day; if they're obligated to provide it regardless of their own will, that obligation is a claim on their body and time that they never agreed to — which is [[Self-ownership|a property claim on someone who owns themselves]], enforceable only by threatening them into compliance. That's not a second flavor of the same kind of right, it's the negation of the first kind: a genuine right can't simultaneously require that someone else be conscripted to satisfy it. Healthcare and education can absolutely be [[Contracts|contracted for voluntarily]] — that's how they already get produced — but calling the result a "right" instead of a "service someone agreed to sell you" is what launders the coercion.
