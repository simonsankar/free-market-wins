The claim: markets replace human concern with cold profit-seeking — businesses chase margins, not welfare, so people get treated as means to an end.

A profit is what you get when you make someone better off *by their own judgment* and they hand over less than that value is worth to them — it's not extracted from people, it's the receipt for having served them. [[Markets|Every trade in a market]] is two parties who each value what they're getting more than what they gave up, or the trade doesn't happen; a business that stops serving people stops making profit, full stop. "Profits over people" treats those as competing goals when profit *is* the measurement of how well you served people, aggregated across everyone who chose to pay you. The actual alternative — deciding output by committee instead of by whether people will voluntarily hand over their own money for it — is what removes the person from the equation, replacing millions of individual judgments about what they value with one central judgment about what they're allowed to have.
