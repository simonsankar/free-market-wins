The claim: water, power, and the internet are too essential, or too foundational to everything else, to be left to for-profit providers — they need to be run as public services instead.

There's no coherent definition of "public good" that isn't just "thing I want subsidized," and "too essential to be for-profit" is the same move dressed up as a safety argument rather than an economic one — food and shelter are more essential than either and nobody proposes nationalizing farms or homebuilders. Essential goods are exactly the ones markets are best at supplying reliably, because demand for them is the most stable and predictable a producer could ask for — that's a reason to expect *heavy* private investment, not an excuse for the state to preempt it. What actually produces the monopoly utility looks like — one grid, one pipe network, no competitor in sight — isn't a natural feature of these markets, it's the result of state-granted franchises and regulatory barriers that make competing illegal in the first place. The market allocates these goods more efficiently and cheaper than central planning ever could, full stop.
