The claim: roads are the classic case for government — who else would build and maintain thousands of miles of infrastructure with no obvious way to charge each user for each trip?

Toll roads, private turnpikes, and HOA/business-district-maintained streets already exist and already charge per-use or bundle the cost into rent, dues, or delivery prices — the "no way to charge" premise is false as a matter of observed fact, not theory. Where direct tolling is impractical, the cost gets bundled the way every other input cost gets bundled: into the price of what the road delivers, same as a store's rent is bundled into the price of its goods rather than charged to you at the door. The actual question was never "can it be funded without taxation" — it demonstrably can — it's "would it be allocated as efficiently as the current tax-funded version," and the honest answer is it would be allocated *more* efficiently, because private owners face real profit-and-loss discipline on maintenance and capacity that a public works department funded by compulsory revenue never has to face. "Muh roads" survives as a meme because it's a vivid mental image, not because anyone's checked whether it's true.
