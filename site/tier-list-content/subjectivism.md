The claim: Austrian economics itself says value is subjective — it exists only in the eye of the valuer — so any rule for assigning property, including homesteading, is just as arbitrary as any other. If nothing has objective value, no property rule can claim to be objectively correct.

This confuses two different words doing two different jobs. [[Subjective Value Theory|"Subjective" describes how much someone wants a thing]] — that varies person to person and is genuinely not arbitrary but relational, the way "tall" depends on what you're comparing to without being meaningless. Whether homesteading is the *correct rule for assigning ownership* is a completely separate question, answered not by anyone's preferences but by which rule avoids generating endless violent conflict over scarce resources: [[Homesteading (First-comer Ethic)|every alternative — latecomer claims, majority vote, common ownership — collapses into paralysis or forced transfer]], while first-use is the only rule that's objectively verifiable (who was there first is a fact, not a preference) and requires no enforcer to constantly adjudicate. "Value is subjective" tells you why people trade; it says nothing about whether force is a legitimate way to reassign what they own, and pretending it does is a category error, not an insight.
