The claim: profit is unpaid labor — a worker produces value greater than their wage, and the capitalist pockets the difference without having produced anything themselves. This is the core Marxist exploitation claim, built on the labor theory of value.

Both halves of the theory it depends on are false. [[Subjective Value Theory|Value isn't a property embedded in an object by the labor that went into it]] — it's assigned by a valuer in relation to their own ends, which is why the same hour of labor can produce something worth a fortune or something worth nothing depending on whether anyone wants it; labor is a cost, not the source of value, and treating it as the latter is the error the entire theory is built on. And the "surplus" isn't unpaid: the capitalist forwent consumption to accumulate the capital that makes the worker's labor productive in the first place, the worker is paid an agreed wage *before* any profit materializes and regardless of whether it ever does, and the capitalist alone eats the loss if the venture fails — see [[Wage Labour Is Righteous, Socialism Is Evil]] for the full case. Calling that arrangement "exploitation" is a bare entitlement claim on someone else's property with no argument underneath it, and abolishing it means violating the very [[private property|property rights]] wage labor and socialism both get tested against.
