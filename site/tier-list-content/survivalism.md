The claim — sometimes called "reification of the zero" — is that NAP-based ethics only ever tells you what you may not do to others (don't kill, don't steal, don't coerce), which means the whole framework is organized around avoiding death and conflict rather than offering any positive vision of what a flourishing human life actually looks like. A philosophy of pure non-violence is a floor, not a purpose.

That's true and not a problem, because the NAP was never sold as a complete personal ethics — it's a political principle answering one specific question: how do we resolve conflicts over scarce resources between people who may have entirely different, even incompatible, visions of a good life? [[The Non-aggression Principle|Its job is to be universalizable across every one of those visions]], which is exactly why it has to stay minimal and say nothing about what you should do with your own life beyond not aggressing against others doing the same. The positive content the objection is looking for exists right alongside it: [[Life the standard for man|life as the standard, from which achievement, reason, and flourishing are all derived]] as what you *ought* to pursue once the floor is secured. Demanding that a boundary condition also be a life plan is asking a property line to also be a home — the NAP clears the ground precisely so a rich ethics of flourishing has somewhere to be built without anyone else's permission.
