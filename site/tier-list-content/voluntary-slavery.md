The claim: if contracts are voluntary and binding, nothing stops someone from selling themselves into permanent bondage — sign the paper, you're property now.

It can't happen, because [[Self-ownership]] isn't a possession you hold, it's what makes you capable of holding anything — including signing a contract in the first place. A contract requires a party who can consent, and consent is an ongoing act of will, not a one-time signature that survives the will that made it. [[Contracts]] lays out why: a permanent, no-exit slavery contract is void the moment it's tested, because to enforce it against someone who has since withdrawn consent, you'd need to already own their body to force compliance — which is exactly the thing in dispute. You can sell your labor, your output, decades of exclusive service. You cannot sell the faculty that makes selling anything possible. That's not a policy limit on contracts, it's what makes the word "contract" mean something instead of being a euphemism for capture.
