The claim: remove the state and the power vacuum gets filled anyway — by warlords, cartels, or mega-corporations who become the new de facto rulers.

Warlordism is how *states* get founded, not how markets behave — a warlord is just a state in its larval stage, someone trying to seize a permanent, unaccountable monopoly on force over a territory. The question is whether that's achievable and profitable in a stateless order, and it isn't: there's no money-printer to fund the conquest, no existing conscript army to inherit, no legal machinery to legitimize the land grab — every gain has to be bought at real cost from real owners who'd rather sell to your defense-agency competitors than to you. See [[Anarcho-Capitalism deters state formation]] for the full mechanics: buying up the competition funds the competition's ability to resist you, and a would-be conqueror who has to answer to paying customers rather than a captive tax base takes on risk no state ever has to. "It'll just turn into warlords" isn't a prediction about anarcho-capitalism, it's a description of how the state you're currently defending was built.
